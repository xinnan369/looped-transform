# Competing explanations

The two-seed B archive separates where a discrepancy appears from why it appears. The separately approved E40 GPU comparison now adds same-parameter gradient evidence. Generated CPU equivalence validated the measurement method before execution.

| Hypothesis | Evidence for | Evidence against or limit | Discriminating observation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shared block call gradients oppose one another | The second shared call has a smaller local nAP gain than independent blocks under both policies and in both seeds overall and at high density. The corresponding EMA nAP correction gap is −.00339 overall. | Shared count correction closes the macro high-density gap by 2.59 heads. The seed directions differ, and the matched independent blocks have different parameter spaces. Archive readouts contain no call-gradient observations. | Measure the two stateless call contributions on the same shared parameter basis for final and total losses. Require output, optimizer-free state and gradient-sum equivalence. Compare negative projection magnitude and consistency across seeds and strata separately from cross-space independent cosines. |
| Early supervision interferes with final supervision | The same block/head receive both gradients in Shared-2. A shared decoder receives both even in Unshared-2. The observed stagewise ranking gap leaves this hypothesis open. | Assistive supervision improved earlier cohorts and both auxiliary arms here use equal weight. No archived loss gradients identify opposing components. | Compare the weighted early vector with the final vector on each actual block/head. Use the decoder and first block of the independent arm as separate references. An early loss path to its second block is absent. Compare supervision results with call-gradient results before choosing a mechanism. |
| Shared capacity limits second-stage localization | Matched Unshared-2 has the larger second-stage nAP gain, with the direction repeated across seeds and policies. | A capacity explanation does not predict the stronger macro shared high-density count correction by itself. Stage-one nAP is already lower. Candidate-level spatial shifts and rank are unavailable. | First separate call direction effects from the stage-one baseline. Only after real evidence, consider a single minimal call-conditioned intervention with equal readout, compute and supervision reporting. |
| First-stage baseline drives much of the count gap | High group starts +4.36 MAE apart under EMA and ends +1.77 after relative correction. Both EMA seeds start behind. | The second-call relative correction differs substantially by seed and also explains +0.69 of the live-to-EMA final-gap widening. A one-stage diagnosis cannot explain the repeated nAP correction deficit. | Independently track early and final loss paths, tensor-space probes and archived paired readouts. Check if strong conflict evidence remains after separating the early gap and high-group cancellation. |
| Count cancellation or ranking affects the contrast | At high density the EMA unmatched FP+FN reduction is 40.8 vs 41.5, while shared cancellation grows by 34.8 vs 5.6. Shared final produces 11.2 additional FP and misses 29.1 additional GT under the count matcher. | The archived pooled nAP gap reflects raw candidate ranking and the matcher independent of the count threshold. No candidate-level identities or location changes are available in these CSVs. | If a later scope authorizes evaluation, freeze a candidate-level spatial/rank diagnostic or record GT identity joins at the same fixed evaluation settings. Keep score floors and cutoffs fixed. |

**Decision after the GPU scope.** The frozen endpoint probe observed positive shared-call projections and positive early/final supervision projections. There were zero negative shared-parameter comparisons, including in the high-density subgroup. The two conflict hypotheses are unsupported at the measured endpoint. Capacity, first-stage baseline and localization/rank explanations remain open. The unmatched-count cancellation analysis explains why MAE can improve while the nAP gap widens. The current evidence does not support a targeted intervention. Candidate-level evidence collected under a separately approved fixed scoring protocol could distinguish matched GT transitions, localization shifts and ranking effects.
